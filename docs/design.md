# The finished game

This is the design for the complete *The Last Camp*: a relaxed first-person story
game of about an hour, built on the existing camp, pond and woodland. It describes
the target. [The roadmap](roadmap.md) orders the work and says how each step is
accepted; [the README](../README.md) describes what plays today. Decisions that only
David can make are listed at the end, each with the default this design uses until
he answers.

## What the game is

Three days at a lakeside camp in a northern mixed forest in the last week of August.
You come to close your grandmother's camp for the last time. You light her fire,
follow her old journal to the places she and your grandfather loved, and on the way
you find out what your grandfather was doing out here alone in his last summer. A
storm comes through. On the last morning you leave the site the way it was before
anyone camped there, and you carry home the one thing June never knew was waiting
for her.

The tone is quiet, warm and a little sad. There is no survival timer, no hunger and
no damage. Challenge is light and physical (a good stone throw, a clean paddle
stroke, a fish landed, a split on the beat), never punishing. The game ends.

Existing work survives in place. The `one_night` film and the showcase reel stay as
offline renders built from the same world, and their camera paths supply the title
background, the chapter cards and the closing cutscene.

## Story

**Setting.** Halloway Pond, a small kettle pond about 34 m across, with a pier, a
canoe and a clearing on its east bank. The camp stands on a state-forest lease that
June and Tom Halloway held from 1974. The lease ends on September 1; after that the
clearing goes back to the forest. A letter from the forest office in June's packet
says that the pier and the heron blind pass to the state forest for public use, so
they stay; everything else the Halloways brought goes home.

**People.**

- **You**: June's grandchild. You are never named, seen or voiced; June calls you "love".
- **June Halloway** (78): she camped here every August for 52 years. Her knees no
  longer manage the trail. She is heard through her journal, read in her voice: the
  long entries she wrote for you, and short margin notes she left beside things she
  knew you would find.
- **Tom Halloway**: June's husband, who died in the autumn of 2014. He built the pier
  and the heron blind and held the stone-skipping record of nine. He is present only
  in June's words, in what he made and in what he wrote. He is never voiced.

**What June asks.** Her letter, left on the camp table with her journal, a pen and
her old film camera, asks three things: "Light the fire one more time. Find our marks
and bring me pictures. And on the last morning, leave it the way we found it." In the
same letter she says, almost in passing: "Tom spent his last summer up here on his
own, watching his herons, he said. I never asked what he was really doing. I wish I
had."

**The dramatic question** is what Tom was doing. It is raised in Chapter 1, sharpened
at the heron blind in Chapter 2, pointed somewhere at the birch grove, and answered
at Tom's Rock in Chapter 3:

- **The blind (Ch2).** Under the bench is Tom's heron tally book. Its 2014 pages count
  herons, and then, in the margins, count something else: "steps: 6 of 20",
  "steps: 14 of 20", and a route sketch June has never seen, marked with his blaze,
  a notched cut instead of her paint.
- **The grove (Ch3).** Beside the 1971 "J + T" carving, one birch has a fresh-looking
  notched blaze, the first mark of Tom's route. The tally book's last page reads:
  "20th step goes under the bench. She'll find it when she sits."
- **Tom's Rock (Ch3).** Tom's route climbs to the lookout by twenty flat stone steps
  he set himself, and ends at a stone bench facing the pond and the sunset. June sat
  on that bench when she scattered his ashes, without knowing what it was. The
  twentieth step is loose. Under it is a tin with Tom's letter for their 40th
  anniversary, August 2014, which he never got to give her.

You read Tom's letter on screen; it is never voiced. You take it home: it is the one
thing you carry out that was not brought in. June's postcard, after the credits, is
her answer to it.

**Arc.**

| Part | Chapter | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginning | 1. Arrival (day 1, evening and night) | You walk in from the trailhead at 18:45, read June's letter, and do what she did on every first night: wood, stones, the sunset from the pier, the fire, the lantern. You sleep in her tent. |
| Middle | 2. The Marks (day 2 and night 2) | You follow the journal to the spring and the old fire ring, take the canoe across to Tom's heron blind and find his tally book, catch and cook supper, and keep the fire alive through a night storm while June tells of the 1996 storm that flattened their tent. |
| End | 3. Tom's Route (day 3 and the last night) | You find the start of Tom's route at the birch grove, follow his blazes and steps up to Tom's Rock, sit on his bench as the sun goes down, and find the tin. The last fire. June's journal has one blank page left, and you write it. |
| Close | Epilogue: Leave No Trace (dawn) | You douse the fire, scatter the ring stones, cover the scar, strike the tent, take down the tarp line, pack the kit and walk out. Looking back from the trailhead starts the closing cutscene over the clearing. Credits, then June's postcard. |

## Voice and text

**Journal entries.** Ten long entries, 30 to 60 seconds each, voiced and subtitled,
and readable again in the journal. An entry starts when its trigger fires and keeps
playing while you walk, as in *Firewatch*; it pauses only for menus.

| # | Entry | Unlocked by | What June tells |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Letter | reading the letter on the table | why you are here; her three requests; Tom's last summer |
| 2 | First Fire | lighting the fire after sunset on night 1 | their first fire in 1974, lit in the rain with a whole box of matches |
| 3 | Nine Skips | a throw of 3 or more skips | Tom's record, and how he picked his stones a week ahead |
| 4 | The Spring | reaching the spring | the cold water; the enamel mug that still hangs there |
| 5 | The First Ring | reaching the old fire ring | where they camped before they knew where the wind came from |
| 6 | The Blind | reaching the heron blind | Tom building it in 1988; the heron that came back every year |
| 7 | The Storm of '96 | sheltering in the tent in the night-2 storm | the night the tent came down and they slept under the canoe |
| 8 | J + T | reaching the birch grove | the initials Tom carved in 1971, before there was a camp |
| 9 | Tom's Rock | sitting on the bench at the lookout | 2014; letting go; "the last page is yours" |
| 10 | The Postcard | finishing the game | her answer to Tom's letter, your photographs and your last page |

**Margin notes.** About 36 short voiced lines (3 to 10 s each, about 4 minutes in all)
that June wrote beside the things you would meet. Each plays once, on the first
matching action, and is written into the journal's margin. They are written ahead
of time, so they read as June knowing you, not as June watching you. The first set:

- Places and objects: the trailhead post, the register box at the trailhead, the
  table, the camera, the tent, the tackle tin's contents (Tom's flat stones, a
  1980s bobber, a fishing licence from 1979), the mug at the spring, Tom's hatchet at
  the woodpile ("leave his hatchet be; the axe in the block does the work"), the
  canoe, the pier's carved step, the bucket, the crate, the forest office letter.
- First actions: first log carried, first split, first glancing blow, first skip,
  first five-skip, first cast, first bite lost, first fish of each species, first
  paddle stroke, first time the canoe drifts, first forage find, first supper, the
  fire dying down, the first rain on the tarp, the first heron call, the first loon,
  waking after the storm (two variants), the walk down from the Rock in the dark.
- Tom's blazes: three short lines as you follow his route ("He never told me about
  these").

**Found texts** are read on screen in the journal's style and are not voiced: Tom's
tally book (four pages), Tom's letter, the forest office letter and the trailhead
register.

**Voice total.** About 7 minutes of entries, 4 minutes of margin notes and up to
2 minutes of postcard variants: about 13 minutes over an hour of play.

## Choices

The choices change words and memories, never what content you can reach.

- **Photograph captions.** Each required photograph is captioned in the journal by
  choosing one of two lines in your voice (for example, for the spring: "Still cold."
  or "The mug's still here.").
- **The storm.** Keeping the fire alive through the storm is a real goal, not a
  requirement. If it goes out, the storm still ends, but the next morning is
  different: the kit is wet, one tarp corner has come down, June's margin note is the
  other one, and her postcard mentions it.
- **The last page.** On the last night you write the final journal page by choosing
  one of three closing lines: one about the fire, one about Tom, one about coming back.
- **June's postcard** is assembled from three paragraphs. The first answers Tom's letter
  (one version). The second reacts to what you brought her: which of the optional
  photographs you took (the carving, the heron, the camp at sunset, the storm fire),
  whether the fire survived the storm, and whether you beat Tom's nine. The third
  answers your last line (three versions). About ten voiced pieces cover the
  combinations.

## How time works

The sky is the existing atmosphere model: solar noon at 13:00 with the sun 62°
up, sunrise 05:45 and sunset 20:15 (`Atmosphere.cs`). That is a high-summer sun,
which is why the story is set in late August rather than late September; the summer
meadow, fireflies and dragonflies already in the world agree with it. The sun model
does not change, so the films and the capture viewpoints keep their light.

Each chapter is split into **bands** of time. Inside a band the clock runs at a rate
chosen so that a player taking the expected time sees the band's whole arc of light.
At the band's end the clock stops ("the afternoon lingers") until the band's
required objectives are done. When they are done, the player moves to the next band
by a deliberate act: resting on the split-log bench by the fire or at the bedroll,
sitting on the pier or on Tom's bench, or turning in at the tent. The fade can
happen as soon as the
objectives are done, so a fast player never waits, and a slow one never runs out of
light. No objective has a deadline. The clock moves only forward; neither photo mode
nor the journal changes it in a chapter.

The sun's height at the times used below: 06:18 about 6°; 09:00 39°; 12:00 60°;
18:30 21°; 18:45 18°; 19:30 9°; 20:00 3°; 20:15 0° (sunset). The sunset itself is
played as a sequence: once you sit, the clock runs from the band's end to 20:30 over
about 90 seconds, with the photograph window open throughout.

| Chapter | Band | Clock | Required in this band | Ends by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Evening | 18:45 → holds at 19:50 | read the letter; carry three logs to the ring; skip a stone 3 times | sitting at the end of the pier (sunset sequence to 20:30) |
| 1 | Night | 20:30 → holds at 23:30 | light the fire; light the lantern | turning in at the tent |
| 2 | Morning | 06:18 → holds at 12:00 | open the tackle tin in the tent; fill the bucket at the spring; find the old fire ring | resting on the log bench (fade to 13:00) |
| 2 | Afternoon | 13:00 → holds at 18:30 | paddle to the heron blind; find Tom's tally book; catch a fish; split three logs | resting on the log bench (fade to 18:45) |
| 2 | Evening | 18:45 → holds at 19:45 | cook supper on the tripod | finishing supper: the wind turns, clouds close in over 30 s, clock to 20:45 |
| 2 | Storm | 20:45 → holds at 23:30 | string the tarp (three tie-offs); feed the fire twice in the rain; shelter in the tent until the storm passes | the storm ends in the tent; sleep |
| 3 | Morning | 06:18 → holds at 12:00 | find the birch grove and Tom's first blaze | resting on the log bench (fade to 18:15) |
| 3 | Evening | 18:15 → holds at 19:40 | follow Tom's route to the Rock | sitting on Tom's bench (sunset sequence to 20:30); lift the 20th step |
| 3 | Night | 20:30 → holds at 23:30 | walk down by lantern; light the last fire; write the last page | sleeping in the tent |
| Epilogue | Dawn | 06:00 → holds at 07:30 | douse the fire; scatter the ring stones and cover the scar; strike the tent; take down the tarp line; pack the crate | walking out past the trailhead post |

"First Fire" belongs to night 1 because the fire is laid but unlit until the night
band: June always lit it "when the first star showed", and the light prompt appears
only then.

## Time budget

Walking speed is 2.9 m/s and running 5.6 m/s (`Player.cs`). Every place is 50 to 85 m
from camp in a straight line, so footpaths are drawn to wind with the ground, as real
footpaths do, at about 1.5 to 2.3 times the straight distance. Even so, walks are
short: the game's length comes from what you do and hear at each place, not from
the walking, and the budget below says so.

| Route | Straight line | Path | Walk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trailhead to camp (existing trail) | 70 m | 75 m | 26 s |
| Camp to the spring | 50 m | 90 m, along a seep line | 31 s |
| Spring to the old fire ring | 60 m | 100 m, round the knoll | 34 s |
| Old fire ring to camp | 54 m | 70 m | 24 s |
| Pier to the blind landing by canoe | about 40 m | across the pond | 45–60 s of paddling |
| Camp to the birch grove | 85 m | 120 m, past the old ring | 41 s |
| Grove to Tom's Rock by Tom's route | 140 m | 230 m, through the south woods and up twenty steps in switchbacks | 80–90 s, slower on the steps |
| Tom's Rock down to camp | 72 m | 110 m, June's painted path | 38 s |

Expected time for a first-time player who does the required objectives, hears every
line and does some optional things (activity times are the designed lengths of each
activity below):

| Chapter | Walking | Activities | Voice and text | Exploring and optional | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Arrival | 1.5 min | wood 1, stones 3, sunset 1.5, fire and lantern 1.5, tent 0.5 | 3 min | 2 min | about 14 min |
| 2. The Marks | 4 min | tin 0.5, bucket 1, canoe 3, fishing 4, splitting 2, supper 2, storm 5 | 6 min | 4 min | about 31 min |
| 3. Tom's Route | 4 min | blazes and steps 2, sunset 1.5, tin 1, last fire 1, last page 1.5 | 5 min | 2 min | about 18 min |
| Epilogue | 1 min | douse 0.5, ring 2, tent 1.5, tarp line 0.5, pack 1 | 1 min | cutscene 1.2, credits 2, postcard 1 | about 11 min |

So the game runs **about 75 minutes** for a typical first play, 50 minutes for a
player who runs and skips optional things (voice keeps playing while you move), and
longer for anyone chasing the collections. The milestone footage measures the real
figure; this table is replaced with measured times at M6 and M9.

## Activities

All of them use the real player controller and the first-person hands. Tools that
stand visibly in the world are usable as soon as their activity exists; the story
gates objectives, not tools.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carry and feed firewood | E to take, E at the fire (A) | existing woodpile and firepit | none | none |
| Split logs | hold left mouse to raise, release on the beat (hold and release RT) | the procedural axe in the chopping block | a glancing blow; swing again | clean-split streak |
| Fire | lit after sunset; feeding keeps its intensity up; it dims slowly | existing firepit intensity | it dims; in the storm it can go out and be relit | fire kept alive through the storm |

| Fish | hold to cast and release; wait for the bobber; hold to reel against a tension meter | pond disturbance field, the rod from the tackle tin | the fish slips off; cast again | biggest fish of each species |
| Fetch water | E at the spring with the credited bucket | credited bucket model | none | none |
| Forage | E on mushrooms and blueberries (12 spots) | habitat fields | none | all 12 |

| Strike the tent | pull four pegs, then the poles; the cloth falls and is rolled | the tent's soft-body cloth | none | none |

**June's camera** in a chapter is held at the player's eye. It frames, zooms from
35 mm to 135 mm, focuses and shoots, and nothing else: no flight, no time scrubbing.
A place counts when its mark is in frame, within 25 m of the player's eye and not
hidden. The free-flying PhotoRig, with its speed control and time scrubbing (T, [ ]),
stays for Free Play and the existing capture tools only.

**The canoe** seats the player: the character controller is suspended, the camera is
parented to the hull, and the hull keeps its Jolt buoyancy. Leaving the canoe is
possible at the pier and at the blind landing. Fish bites and species come from a
seeded generator, so bot runs are repeatable.

## Places

The walking area stays the existing 120 m radius around (-10, 8). Everything below is
inside it, so no boundary changes are needed. Directions follow the engine: north is
-Z and east is +X (`Atmosphere._dir_from_angles`); June's ink map has a north arrow
on the same convention. New footpaths join the existing trail as polylines in the
terrain's trail field, and placement keeps the existing support and route
constraints.

| Place | Approximate centre (x, z) | Direction from camp | Status | What is there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Camp clearing | (0, 0) | on the pond's east bank | exists | fire, tent, table, woodpile, chopping block, split-log bench, crate |
| Pond and pier | pond (-34, 4), pier from (-15.9, 6.3) | west | exists | stones, moored canoe |
| Trailhead | (4, 70), the trail's south end | south | new post | carved post "HALLOWAY 1974", register box; the epilogue walk-out |
| The Spring | (30, -40) | north-east | new | moss boulder, rock basin, a slow seep, the enamel mug on a nail |
| Old fire ring | (-30, -45) | north, a little west | new | overgrown ring of stones and a fallen log seat |
| Heron blind | far shore, about (-56, 10) | west, across the pond | new | a plank hide with a slot window on the reeds; reached by canoe or the long way round |
| Birch grove | (-60, -60) | north-west | new | about a dozen birches; one carved "J + T 1971"; Tom's first blaze |
| Tom's Rock | highest walkable ground in the south-east sector, 60–110 m out, near (40, 60) | south-east | new | granite outcrop with Tom's twenty steps and stone bench, looking north-west over the pond into the sunset |

From (40, 60) the pond lies at a bearing of about 307°, and the sun sets at about
289°, so the sunset sits 18° left of the pond in the lookout's view. Tom's Rock is
placed by a probe that finds the highest supported point with a clear view of the
pond. If no point inside the radius sees the water, the radius gets one corridor
along Tom's route, and the boundary checks are extended to match.

## Progression and unlocks

- **Chapters** gate in order: Arrival, The Marks, Tom's Route, and the Epilogue. Places
  can be visited at any time; their entries play when you first arrive, and Tom's
  blazes appear on the map only after the tally book is found.
- **Story items** come from the story: the tackle tin (rod, bobber and Tom's stones)
  on day 2; the tarp from the crate on night 2; the tally book at the blind; Tom's
  letter at the Rock.
- **Collections** are shown in the journal: 10 entries, the margin notes, 7 required
  and 4 optional photographs with their captions, 3 fish species (perch, sunfish,
  brook trout) with the biggest of each, 12 forage finds, the best split streak, the
  quiet crossing, and the stone-skip best against Tom's 9.
- **After the credits**, "Return to Halloway Pond" opens Free Play with every
  activity unlocked and the collections kept. Its pause menu has a time-of-day
  selector, and P opens the free-flying photo rig. Chapter Select replays any chapter
  from its start.

## Win and lose

- **Win**: finish the epilogue. The closing cutscene, credits and June's postcard play,
  then a summary card (entries, photographs, species and biggest fish, forage, best
  skip, the storm fire) and the title menu, with Free Play and Chapter Select unlocked.
- **No lose state** is proposed: the stakes are the soft ones above, the storm fire's
  consequences and the records. David's goal asks every game for win and lose
  states, so this departure is put to him (see the decisions at the end).
- **Nothing is missable**: every optional collectible stays reachable in Free Play.
- **Never stuck.** Fall recovery restores the last supported position, as now. The
  canoe returns to its mooring. The pause menu has "Return to camp", which fades to
  the fire.

## Title, menus, pause, settings and saves

- **Title**: the title lettering and a menu over a still of the camp at dusk while the
  world builds, then a live view on an existing camera path fades in, with the fire's
  sound and wind. Menu: Continue (with the chapter and in-game time), New Game (asks
  before overwriting), Chapter Select and Free Play (after finishing), Settings,
  Credits, Quit.
- **Tool runs** skip the title: `--session-check`, `--benchmark`, `--profile`,
  `--traverse`, `--cinematic` and the other capture flags start straight into the
  world as they do now.
- **Pause** (Esc or Start): Resume, Journal, Settings, Return to camp, Save and quit to
  title, Quit to desktop. Free Play adds the time-of-day selector.
- **Settings**, saved to `user://settings.cfg`:
  - Graphics: preset (Low, Medium, High, Ultra), adaptive quality on or off, field of
    view 60–90°, motion blur and head-bob on or off.
  - Audio: master, ambience, effects and voice volumes.
  - Subtitles on or off, at three sizes.
  - Controls: mouse and stick sensitivity, invert Y, hold or toggle for charging a
    throw and casting, an assisted split timing window, and the player's pencil mark
    on June's map.
  - Key rebinding is out of scope; the controls page lists the keyboard and pad
    bindings.
- **Gamepad**: every action has a pad binding, and prompts show the glyphs of the last
  device used.
- **Saves**: one story slot, `user://save/story.json`, versioned. It holds the chapter
  and band, each objective's state, the clock, weather, fire intensity, story items,
  collections and captions, the ring's state, the tent's state, the storm outcome,
  the chosen last line and the last safe position. It autosaves on sleep, at each band
  change, on discovering a place and on "Save and quit". A write goes to a temporary
  file and is then renamed, so a crash never corrupts the slot. If a write fails (a
  full disk, for example), the old slot is kept and a warning says the game was not
  saved. The stone-skip record moves into the save (migrated once from
  `user://camp-records.cfg`). Continue resumes at the last safe position (camp, the
  pier or a place), with the canoe at its mooring.

## Interface

- **Journal**: a two-page book. The left page holds the objectives, the entry text and
  the margin notes; the right page holds June's ink map (rendered in code from the
  terrain field and the trail polylines, with her marks stamped as you find them,
  Tom's route added in pencil once you have his book, an optional pencil mark for
  you and a small canoe at its current position) and the photo strip with captions.
  It pauses the game, as now.
- **Objective tracker**: one or two lines at the top left that fade after 6 s and come
  back on change.
- Interaction prompts with key and pad glyphs; a reel-tension arc and a throw-charge
  arc; the camera viewfinder with a "mark in frame" tick; chapter title cards;
  subtitles.
- **Style**: paper, ink and canvas: warm off-white, charcoal and a single rust accent.
  The title lettering is drawn by a committed generator as stroke paths, not a font.
  Body text uses a bundled serif (see the decisions at the end). The current HUD text
  becomes this system.

## Art direction

Semi-realistic late-summer woodland, lit by sun, fire and lantern. It stays grounded:
props rest on real supports and wear matches use. Every new asset comes from a
committed generator.

- **Blender generators** (`tools/blender/*.py`, output in `models/generated/`):
  - first-person hands and forearms, rigged, with poses and animations: carry a log,
    hold a stone, hold the lantern, paddle, rod and reel, hold a fish, camera, axe
    swing, bucket, tarp, a ring stone, the tin and the letter;
  - catchable fish for the hands and the journal: perch, sunfish and brook trout,
    each at two or three sizes (the existing PondFish stay as the small shader
    minnows on their routes);
  - rod, reel, line and bobber; tackle tin and its contents (flat stones, the old
    bobber, the licence);
  - forage: two mushroom species and a blueberry bush with picked and unpicked
    states, for 12 spots;
  - the tarp cloth's mesh, its grommets, tie points and lines;
  - on the camp table: June's letter, the journal book, a pen and the film camera;
    the enamel mug for the spring;
  - the trailhead post with the carved lettering and the register box; the heron
    blind and its bench; spring basin rocks; the old fire ring; Tom's twenty steps,
    his stone bench and the loose step; Tom's tally book and his tin;
  - the fire-ring stones as separate physics bodies with collision shapes;
  - the tent interior: bedroll, sleeping bag, June's kit bag; the struck and rolled
    tent;
  - the carved birch trunk section, Tom's notched blaze and June's painted blaze.
- **Python texture generators** (alongside `tools/bake_textures.py`): canvas and tarp
  cloth, weathered planks, carving and blaze normal and ambient-occlusion maps, the
  journal paper and ink map, the letters and tally book pages, the enamel chips on
  the mug, the fish skins, and the ash-scar and duff decal.
- **Rework**: broadleaf canopies read as chunky leaf cards up close. Re-bake the oak
  and birch leaf atlases with smaller clusters, and raise the near-tree card count
  within the performance budget. Light the tent interior from the lantern.
- **Credited photoscans** (35 models, 9 PBR sets) stay until a generated replacement
  is at least as good in play. The credited hatchet stays as Tom's hatchet at the
  woodpile.
- **David's standing visual direction** for this game: never cull trees hard, keep the
  view distance maximal, and no visible fakes even in the distance. High and Ultra
  keep the full view distance; only Medium and Low may trade it.
- **Cutscenes**: the closing cutscene is a new Cinematic timeline (`farewell`, about
  70 s). The title view and chapter cards reuse IntroDolly and the existing camera
  paths. The v1.0.0 film files are frozen release artifacts made with Godot 4.7.2;
  they are not expected to reproduce. The `one_night` and `showcase` generators are
  kept working because the game's cinematics use them, and any change to places,
  paths or vegetation re-renders the affected shots for review.

## Audio direction

- **Soundscape first.** Wind, water, fire, birds, insects, rain, Foley and June's voice
  carry the game, as they carry the film. Whether the title and credits get a short
  theme from the SoundFont pipeline is David's decision (see the end); the default
  is no music anywhere. `scripts/audio/CampScore.cs`, the retained synthesis study,
  stays as a tested generator that the game does not play.
- **Voice**: the local voice kit through a new `tools/voice.py`, copying the kids'
  games' working pattern in full: June's voice is designed once and its reference
  committed with its settings; every line is cloned from that reference with the
  Qwen base model, rendered sentence by sentence, and gated by Whisper word
  matching and speaker similarity; subtitle timings come from Whisper's word
  timestamps. The tool writes OGG files and subtitle timings to `audio/voice/`. The
  voice is warm and elderly with a slight rasp.
- **New synthesised sounds**, made in `Synth.cs` and AudioDirector:
  - activities: paddle strokes and drips, the canoe's knock on the hull, reel ratchet,
    line whip, bobber plop, a fish splash and flap, axe chop and split, tarp flap and
    tie-off, peg pull and cloth fall, stone set-down, ash rake, bucket fill and
    pour, fire hiss when doused, pot simmer;
  - interface: page turn, pencil, camera shutter and wind-on, soft wooden clicks;
  - wildlife: loon and heron calls.
  The existing credited recordings stay.
- **Mix**: voice ducks ambience by 6 dB. Everything goes through the shared room and
  distance model already used for the world. Over a 10-minute gameplay capture the
  mix targets −20 LUFS integrated (±2) with true peak at or below −1 dBTP. Voice
  lines are levelled to −19 LUFS (±1) each, with true peak at or below −3 dBTP.

## Performance targets

On the RTX 4060 Laptop at 1920×1080 output, in the exported build with
`DOTNET_TieredCompilation=0`, along the 22 s benchmark route (`BENCH_PATH` and
`BENCH_LOOK` in `CaptureTool.cs`) and a new full-loop route through every place,
committed beside it. Each preset keeps at least today's internal resolution, so a
target cannot be met by lowering it:

| Preset | Internal scale, at least | Mean | 99th percentile | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 50%, FSR2 | 75 FPS (13.3 ms) | ≤ 18 ms | low power |
| Medium (proposed default) | 67%, FSR2 | 60 FPS (16.7 ms) | ≤ 22 ms | shipping default |
| High | 77%, FSR2 | 45 FPS (22.2 ms) | ≤ 30 ms | quality option |

The gaps today ([measured](validation.md#measured-performance)), all in the exported
build:

| Preset | Today mean / p99 | Mean to find | p99 to find | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 19.17 / 22.07 ms | 5.9 ms | 4.1 ms | measured before streaming, contact shadows and atlas release |
| Medium | 28.84 / 37.31 ms | 12.1 ms | 15.3 ms | measured before streaming, contact shadows and atlas release |
| High | 34.63 / 46.02 ms | 12.4 ms | 16.0 ms | current build |

Low and Medium are remeasured on `master` at M0 before any of these gaps is used for
planning. The measured levers are grass and understory submission (4–8 ms), the
planar reflection (3–6 ms of total frame time), forest shadow casters (2–4 ms) and
near-tree geometry.

Also required:

- No frame over 100 ms in a 30-minute soak.
- Peak process working set at or below 6 GB and renderer memory at or below 3 GB.
  The working set already peaked at 6.12 GB across the sequential presets, so this
  needs work too.
- The title menu responds within 15 s of launch, and the live title view and a new
  game are ready within 45 s. Startup is already 44.4 s with warm-up, which is why the
  title is shown while the world builds.
- No crash, core dump or NVIDIA Xid in any acceptance run.

Every benchmark JSON records the internal scale and the TieredCompilation setting.
If 60 FPS on Medium needs a visible loss in quality, the options and their measured
costs go to David before a default is cut.

## Scope cuts

Out of scope, on purpose: multiple save slots; key rebinding; swimming in deep water;
capsizing; crafting; inventory management beyond the carried item; other weather
events; places beyond those listed; platforms other than Linux x86-64; a web build;
a voiced player; a voiced Tom.

If the schedule slips, the [roadmap's cut order](roadmap.md#cut-order) says what
goes first. Tom's through-line, the storm, Tom's Rock and the ending are never cut.
