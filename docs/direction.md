# Direction

One page that judges everything in *The Last Camp*. When a screen, a sound, a
motion or a line does not fit it, that thing is redone or removed, even if it passes
its tests.

## The feeling

You leave the game the way you leave a place you will not see again: quiet, a
little sore, and glad you went. It is warm without being sweet. Grief is in the
room but never named. The player is trusted to notice things; nothing is pointed
at.

## Touchstones

- **Firewatch**: a voice in your ear while you walk, and a wilderness that is beautiful
  without showing off.
- **What Remains of Edith Finch**: a family told through the things they left.
- **Andrew Wyeth's late-summer tempera**: dry grass, low sun, worn wood and an empty
  chair. The palette and the restraint.

## Palette and light

- **Day**: late August. Dry gold-green grass, grey-brown bark, mid-green leaves going
  tired at the edges. The sky is pale and hazy, never saturated blue.
- **Golden hour** is the game's signature: low sun, long shadows, warm rim light on
  grass tips. Most story beats happen in it.
- **Night**: blue-black, not green-grey. The fire is the warmest, brightest thing on
  screen and lights the faces of the logs, the ground and the near trees in a
  readable circle. The lantern carries a small warm pool. Darkness beyond is honest
  dark, not fog.
- **Wet**: rain darkens and glosses surfaces evenly. Puddles sit only where the
  ground dips. No blotchy gloss on flat ground.
- **Grass and leaves take backlight**: at golden hour they glow with a translucent rim
  where the sun is behind them.
- **Grass** reads as soft, dense, varied blades with bright tips and dark roots, never
  as flat dark straps. Close-up blades are thin and tapered. If a blade cannot look
  right at arm's length, it is not drawn there.

## Typeface and UI kit

- **One serif**: Noto Serif, bundled with its licence, for menus and all printed text.
  One scale at 1080p: 18 (small print, the floor), 22 (body), 30 (heading), 56 (title).
- **Two hands**: June's handwriting (her letter and her margin notes) is a real,
  legible script face; Tom's is blockier, in pencil grey. Settings has a plain-text
  toggle that sets both in the serif.
- **Colours**: paper `#EFE7D6`, ink `#2A2622`, rust accent `#9A4A2A`, faded ink
  `#6E655A` for secondary text. No other UI colour.
- **One panel**: paper with a soft deckled edge and a faint ink border, no glass, no
  blur, no dark translucent boxes.
- **One button**: ink text on paper; hover and focus underline it in rust with a 120 ms
  ease. No icons, no rounded pills.
- **The journal is the game's one interface.** Tasks, the sense of where things are,
  and the story all live in it: a two-page book on the same paper. There are no
  objective markers, no compass and no minimap.
- **In play** the screen is almost empty: a small interaction dot and one verb
  ("Take", "Read", "Sit") when something can be used, and subtitles. No clock and no
  counters.

## Hands

The player's sleeves and hands belong to someone in their thirties or forties:
weathered, bare, a worn flannel cuff. Hands appear only when holding or doing
something, and every held object has its own grip pose. Nothing floats.

## Pace

Walking is the game's speed. Nothing times the player except the sun and the storm,
and the sun waits at each of June's places until her entry has finished.

## Camera and motion

- First person at eye height. Head-bob is gentle and can be turned off.
- Everything eases: menus and fades ease out over 250–400 ms, cards and the journal
  open over 300 ms, nothing snaps.
- The camera settles; it never shakes, except one soft low thunder rumble in the
  storm (under 1° and under 0.5 s).
- Held items sway with walking and lag the look by a few frames, then settle.
- Fades between bands and chapters go through black over 1 s, with sound carrying
  across them.

## Sound

- Every sound is generated by ElevenLabs and listed in the repository's audio
  manifest. No sound is made in code.
- Silence is specific, not empty. Each time of day has its own bed: the day woods with
  distant birds; evening, with the birds thinning and the first crickets; night
  insects by the pond and an owl far off; and wind in the pines before the rain.
- Footsteps by surface are the most detailed sounds in the game.
- Every action answers with a sound: picking up, setting down, the lantern, a page,
  the stone in the hand and on the water, each footstep by surface.
- June's voice is close, dry and unhurried, levelled the same everywhere. Ambience
  dips under her by about 6 dB.
- No music in play. One piece is allowed, over the credits. The silence on Tom's
  climb stays silence.

## Five nevers

1. Never point at the story: no markers, glowing outlines or arrows on the step, the
   blazes or the tin.
2. Never fail the player: no fail screen, timer, damage or lost progress.
3. Never tell the player what to feel: no line that explains a feeling, no swelling
   cue, no slow motion, no fade to white at the step.
4. Never break the paper and ink UI with a default Godot control, a dark glass panel
   or a second font.
5. Never let the forest look fake: no hard tree culling, no visible pop-in, no flat
   cards up close.
