# Script

Every line June speaks in the micro cut, and the texts read on screen. June is 78,
plain and warm, dry rather than sentimental; she calls the player "love". She wrote
all of this before you came, so she speaks as someone who knows you, not as someone
watching you. Tom is never voiced.

Entries play when their trigger fires and keep playing while you walk. Margin notes
play once, on the first matching moment, and are written into the journal's margin.

## Entries

Each entry is a page June headed for an occasion before you came. She is answering
what she expected you to do, not watching you do it. Once Tom's tin is open she has
nothing more to say until the lookout: she never saw what is inside it, and the
climb is yours alone with his handwriting.

### 1. "For when you find the table"

*Trigger: reading June's letter on the camp table.*

Well, you found the table, so you found the camp. I'm sorry about the trail. It used
to be shorter, or I used to be younger.

I want three things, love. Light the fire one more time. Find our marks, the places
your grandfather and I kept going back to. And on the last morning, leave it the way
we found it. The state wants it back by the first, and they're welcome to it, as long
as it goes back clean.

Tom spent his last summer up here on his own. Watching his herons, he said. I never
asked what he was really doing. I wish I had.

### 2. "For when you get three"

*Trigger: a throw of three skips or more.*

Three. That's a start.

Tom's record was nine. He picked his stones a week ahead and kept them in his tackle
tin like they were something precious. Flat, a bit heavier than you'd think, and
never from the water's edge, because the edge ones are always wet and round.

I never beat him. I'm not sure I tried very hard. It was nice to watch him be pleased.

### 3. "For the first fire"

*Trigger: lighting the fire after the first star, night 1.*

We lit our first fire here in the rain, in 1974. A whole box of matches. Tom swore
at every one of them, very politely.

I always waited for the first star. It isn't a rule. It's just that if you light it
in daylight you don't see it catch, and the catching is the best part.

Keep it small. A fire's for sitting by, not for showing off.

### 4. "For the old ring"

*Trigger: reaching the old fire ring.*

This is where we camped the first summer, before we knew where the wind came from.
The smoke followed us all week. We moved down to the pond the next year and never
came back up here except to sit.

The log was already lying there then. Tom said it was a bench that hadn't been told
yet.

### 5. "For the lookout"

*Trigger: sitting on the flat stone at the lookout.*

I came up here in 2014 with his ashes, because it was the highest place I could
still climb to and you can see the whole pond. I sat on this rock and I thought how
lucky, how lucky that there's a flat stone just here, the right height.

I didn't let him go all at once. A little every year. I brought the last of him up
last autumn, and it took me most of the afternoon, and I knew on the way down I
wouldn't manage it again. That's why it's you.

Sit a while, love. The sun's in no hurry, and neither was he.

### 6. "For a bad night"

*Trigger: sheltering in the tent in the storm.*

In 1996 a storm like this one took the tent right down on top of us. Tom held up the
middle pole with both hands for an hour, saying it was fine. It was not fine.

We spent the rest of that night under the canoe, on the wet ground, laughing like
idiots. I don't remember being cold. I'm sure I was.

The fire will do what it does. You'll be all right in there.

### 7. The Postcard

*Trigger: after the credits. Written after you told her; one line chosen by the storm.*

Love. You found it. Forty years, and he goes and builds it into a hill.

I've read it more times than I'll tell you.

*If the fire survived:* You kept the fire through the storm. He'd have liked that.
He never once managed it.

*If the fire went out:* The fire went out in the storm, I expect. It always did. He
never once managed to keep it either.

Thank you for leaving it clean. Come and tell me about the herons.

## Margin notes

Short, specific, dry. Each is one or two sentences, written beside things June knew
you would meet. None follows the opening of Tom's tin until the morning after the
storm.

## Read on screen, not voiced

**June's letter.** The text of entry 1, in her hand, with her three requests set out.

**Tom's tally book** (four pages):

1. *Herons, 2014.* A column of dates from June to August, a tally beside each.
2. *July 9*: "2 herons, the old one and a young one." In the margin: "steps: 6 of 20."
3. *July 30*: "Old one only." In the margin: "steps: 14 of 20," and a pencil sketch of
   a route from the old fire ring, up the south slope, marked with a notch.
4. *August 19*: "20th step goes under the bench. She'll find it when she sits."

**Tom's letter**, from the tin under the twentieth step, dated August 2014:

> June,
>
> Forty years. I wanted to give you something you couldn't carry home, so I made you
> a way up. Twenty steps, one for every two years, and a bench at the top facing the
> pond, the right height for you.
>
> I know your knees. Take them slowly. The last step is the one under the bench, and
> when you sit, this will be right under your feet.
>
> I've loved every one of these summers. You were the reason for all of them.
>
> Tom

## The six-line audition

Rendered first, for David's ear, before any other line. Three candidate voices read
the same six lines. Direction: a woman in her late seventies, unhurried, dry, amused at
herself, never tearful; no music under it.

1. Entry 1, the first paragraph ("Well, you found the table...").
2. Entry 5, the second paragraph ("I didn't let him go all at once...").
3. Margin note m3 ("They all do that at first.").
4. Margin note m2 ("Take them from the top...").
5. Entry 6, the second paragraph ("We spent the rest of that night under the canoe...").
6. Entry 7, the first two sentences ("Love. You found it...").
